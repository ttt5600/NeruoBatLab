# Edge lives in timing, not graphs

The notes contain no peer-reviewed evidence of a persistent, after-vig, post-publication edge on US closing game lines. The best-identified mispricings are real but smaller than the vig: momentum overreaction from open to close (Moskowitz, JF 2021) and NFL rest/bye mispricing (Lopez & Bliss 2024). The best-known anomalies, such as NFL home underdogs and high-total unders, have decayed toward 50%. The methods with credible out-of-sample betting evidence are not graph methods. They are three things: making the model *decorrelated from the book* (Hubáček et al. 2019), *selecting models on calibration rather than accuracy* (Walsh & Joshi 2024), and *strong rating features fed to logistic regression or GBM*. The surveyed work contains no market-tested evidence that GNNs, Bayesian-network structure learning, causal discovery (PC/GES/NOTEARS) or graphical lasso add ROI. The one market-tested GNN lost to Pinnacle and made money only inside a subset whose threshold the authors tuned. For player props, no academic paper measures over/under or star-shading bias. The "bet unders" thesis comes entirely from affiliates and pick sellers. On data, **10+ seasons of game lines are free (SBRO 2007–2022, nflverse 1999+, spreadspoke 1979+), but no vendor sells more than about 6 seasons of per-game props**. SportsDataIO starts in 2020 and The Odds API in May 2023, because no vendor backfills. These findings fit the project's own results. A null from a 252-trial prop loop is what a near-efficient market predicts. The Kalshi temperature pass fits the pattern the literature does support: a structural information or timing edge in a thin market, checked on a sealed holdout.

## No published US closing-line edge survives the vig

The strongest rigorous study is Moskowitz's. Using spreads for NFL 1985–2013, NBA 1999–2013, NHL 1995–2013 and MLB 2004–2013, plus open/close lines with bet counts from 2005–2013, he finds "strong evidence of momentum and weaker evidence of value effects that move prices from the open to the close of betting, which are then completely reversed by the game outcome". He also states that these returns "are not large enough to overcome transactions costs" ([NYU Stern PDF](https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/finance/docs/pdfs/Seminars/moskowitz_seminar_paper.pdf)). Search summaries put the momentum Q5−Q1 return at **4.73%/yr (Sharpe 0.73, t = 5.32)**, but those table figures were not checked against the PDF ([JF version](https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2021/08/AssetPricingandSportsBetting_JF.pdf)). The practical point is that the close already contains the overreaction. Fading momentum at the close is roughly breakeven before vig. Only beating the move, which is what CLV measures, captures anything.

Rest effects show a market that *over*-learns. Lopez & Bliss studied 5,679 NFL games from 2002–2023. They find the bye advantage fell from **+2.21 points before the 2011 CBA to +0.31 after**, while the market's implied bye value rose from **+0.39 to +0.97**. Even so, fading the bye edge since 2011 covers only about 52%, below the 52.38% break-even ([arXiv 2408.10867](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10867); [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-economics/articles/10.3389/frbhe.2024.1479832/full)). The famous circadian result is that West Coast teams covered 66% of night games against East Coast teams from 1970–2011. It is a single in-sample study, and the notes found no ATS replication ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/11/131127115357.htm)).

Published anomalies follow a consistent decay curve. NFL home underdogs covered **58.1% in 1973–79, 52.5% in 1981–96, and about 49–51% more recently** ([UNI ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.uni.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1538&context=hpt)). Levitt's 57.7% home-underdog pick rate rests, as he warns, on "only 236 games of a single season" ([Levitt PDF](https://pricetheory.uchicago.edu/levitt/Papers/LevittWhyAreGamblingMarkets2004.pdf)). The favourite-longshot bias depends on the league. College football and basketball moneylines show a classic FLB, but the effect size was paywalled ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v63y2017icp233-239.html)). In MLB the bias appears in opening odds and disappears by the close, according to a thesis snippet ([Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/81e73570-084b-4e39-84d3-ccb4649c61ab/download)). Weather is the one anomaly with an explicit out-of-sample win above 52.38%. That is Borghesi's NFL totals strategy, but it only uses data through 2004 ([T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09603100701335432)). Taken together, any edge that remains is *structural*. It comes from timing (openers, low-attention games), from information the close cannot contain (in-game weather, late lineup news), and from thin markets (college moneylines, MLB openers). Krieger & Davis support the attention point: across 3,756 NFL games from 2007–2021, lines move more in less-visible games, which suggests the openers there are less efficient ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jecfin/v48y2024i2d10.1007_s12197-023-09656-5.html)).

The ranking points the same way throughout. Every credible profit claim comes from information or structure the line underweights, plus honest probability estimates. None comes from a more expressive model on the public box-score features the book already prices. Large comparisons support this. Wilkens found in tennis that "most of the relevant information is embedded in the betting markets", and that returns are "mainly negative over the longer term" across models and staking rules ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3506302)). Hubáček's MathSport review found that GBM on rating features beat PageRank and a hybrid BN on RPS; that attribution is from a secondary snippet ([MathSport 2019](http://ida.felk.cvut.cz/zelezny/pubs/MathSport2019a.pdf)).

## Graph methods find redundancy, not edge, unless the line is a node

The user asked whether graph models can reveal which columns correlate with edge. The direct answer is that a graph built only over features finds feature-to-feature redundancy and says nothing about edge. A graph that includes the outcome and the market line reduces to a line-conditional screen, which the notes recommend running directly. Graphical lasso and PCA are worth using to de-duplicate. Rolling means and differences produce clusters of near-identical columns that split stability selection, and PCR/PLS-style compression beat unrestricted OLS in finance mainly by limiting overfitting ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25398/w25398.pdf)).

Causal discovery is a poor fit here, for reasons the notes document. Reisach, Seiler & Weichwald show that the benchmark success of continuous structure learners is largely "varsortability": a trivial sort-by-variance baseline matches them. On standardised data they fail to recover the true DAG ([arXiv 2102.13647](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.13647)). NOTEARS is not scale-invariant, so rescaling a variable can change the learned graph ([arXiv 2104.05441](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.05441)). Sports features also break faithfulness, because many are deterministic functions of one another. With a few thousand rows and tiny effects, conditional-independence tests have little power. The notes found no paper that applies PC, FCI, GES, LiNGAM or NOTEARS to betting features with an ROI or CLV evaluation. The one defensible graph product is the outcome's **Markov blanket conditional on the market line**, checked by bootstrap stability. In practice that is a stability-selected, line-offset lasso.

The binding constraint is power, not method. The partial correlation of a feature with the market residual has t ≈ ρ√n, so a true ρ = 0.03 gives t ≈ 1.7 at n = 3,000 and t ≈ 3 only near n = 10,000 (derived in the notes). Low-power selectors (stability selection, model-X knockoffs) "select few features in practice" at low SNR ([arXiv 2602.00218](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00218); [knockoffs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.5609)). That is the right behaviour: if nothing survives beyond the line, nothing is there. The strikeout-props backtest's "context adds nothing" result is what this machinery should be expected to produce on an efficient market.

## Honest evaluation: CLV slope, calibration, deflation, fractional Kelly

**CLV is the fast metric, but only where the close is sharp.** Buchdahl regressed realised yield on expected yield implied by Pinnacle's close across 87,960 soccer odds pairs and found a slope "almost exactly 1.00", less the margin ([football-data.co.uk](https://football-data.co.uk/blog/pinnacle_efficiency.php)). Kaunitz et al. bet soft-book outliers against the closing consensus. The strategy was profitable in a 10-year simulation and in live betting, and the authors' accounts were restricted ([arXiv 1710.02824](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824)). No one has published a CLV-vs-profit slope for US player props. ESPN prop `last_updated` timestamps fall after kickoff, so a soft prop "close" can reward agreement with stale lines. The project should therefore estimate the slope on its own data. If the slope is near 1, CLV can be trusted for that market. If it is well below 1, only realised outcomes count.

**Select on proper scores and check calibration on the bets actually placed.** A betting rule acts on p_model − p_book, so overconfidence produces fake edges that then get bet heavily. That is the mechanism behind Walsh & Joshi's result ([arXiv 2303.06021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.06021)). Global calibration can hide miscalibration in the tails, which is where the model disagrees with the book. The decorrelation diagnostic from Hubáček is corr(p_model − p_book, outcome − p_book). A model has edge only if its residual against the book is predictive ([IJF 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016920701930007X)).

**Deflate for the search.** Harvey, Liu & Zhu set the bar for a new factor at **t > 3.0**, with t ≈ 3.9 controlling the family-wise error rate at 5% ([RFS](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/29/1/5/1843824)). The Deflated Sharpe Ratio and CSCV-based Probability of Backtest Overfitting turn this into a procedure on a time × strategy return matrix ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)). The expected maximum of N independent null t-stats is about √(2 ln N): **3.03 at N = 100, 3.33 at N = 252, 3.72 at N = 1,000** (computed). The 252-trial prop loop would therefore have needed its best variant above roughly t = 3.3 just to beat chance. Correlation among trials lowers the effective N somewhat, but not enough to change the conclusion. White's Reality Check and Hansen's SPA are the right family-wise tests. The notes describe them from background knowledge only and have not verified them against the primary sources. Resample by week or season blocks, because same-game props share game script and weather.

**Sample size explains why outcomes alone cannot settle a prop model.** At −110 the per-bet SD is about 0.95 units. Detecting a 2% ROI with 80% power at one-sided α = 0.05 needs about **14,000 independent bets**. A 3% ROI needs about 6,200 and a 5% ROI about 2,250. A t ≥ 3 bar roughly doubles each figure (derived in the notes; consistent with the project's earlier "~4.5 seasons to confirm 2%").

**Size bets with shrunk edges and fractional Kelly.** The growth of a fraction c of true Kelly is c(2 − c) of the maximum. Half Kelly keeps 75% of the growth. If the edge estimate is inflated 2×, as selection makes likely, "full Kelly" yields zero growth (derived; consistent with [MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227623956_Long-term_capital_growth_the_good_and_bad_properties_of_the_Kelly_and_fractional_Kelly_capital_growth_criteria)). Shrink the edge toward zero first, then bet ¼–½ Kelly with a per-game cap for correlated props. Walsh & Joshi's −75.9% Kelly path shows how much damage overbetting a miscalibrated model does.

**Prop economics cut both ways.** No academic paper measures over/under bias, star shading or recency in US player props. The claim that "inefficiencies are more prevalent in player prop … markets" is a framing remark in a student thesis ([CMC thesis](https://scholarship.claremont.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5145&context=cmc_theses)). Any "under bias" test also has to separate out skew. If a book sets the line at the mean of a right-skewed count stat, the under wins more than 50% with no behavioural bias at all, so the comparison should be against the model's own P(X < line), not against 50%. Vendors say props hold 6–9% (**vendor** ([propsbot](https://propsbot.ai/glossary/hold-percentage/))). Yet −115/−105 holds 4.50%, slightly less than −110/−110's 4.55% (computed). Real costs are set per price pair and per book, so line shopping matters more than any single "prop hold" figure. PrizePicks 5-pick Flex breaks even at **54.25% per leg**, equivalent to −118.6 ([GamedayMath](https://www.gamedaymath.com/blog/prizepicks-math)). Execution is the last constraint. BetMGM limits about 1% of Massachusetts patrons and lists a "better model" among its reasons ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/sports-betting/story/_/id/41231266/espn-sports-betting-news-sportsbooks-defend-practice-limiting-sharp-customers)). A backtested edge at retail books is therefore an upper bound. Exchanges and prediction markets do not limit winners in the same way, but their prop books are thin ([Novig](https://novig.com/blog/novig-vs-kalshi-vs-polymarket), self-description).

## Conclusion

The literature and the project's own results agree. A model that learns from public box scores against an efficient close reconstructs the line and loses the vig, however expressive it is. Graph and structure-learning methods do not change that, because they rearrange the same information. Their honest uses are de-duplication and encoding one specific structural hypothesis. Edge has come from *information the line lacks at decision time* or from *markets too thin to have absorbed it*. The Kalshi result fits both. The productive question for the next phase is therefore not which columns correlate with edge, but where and when the price is set before the information arrives. Two caveats apply. The prop literature is effectively empty, so a well-powered, deflated null on 3–6 seasons of multi-book props would itself be informative, since nobody has published one. And with a ~6-season ceiling on prop data, any prop edge smaller than about 3% ROI cannot be confirmed from outcomes. CLV against a validated sharp close is the only feasible test of a prop edge that size.
