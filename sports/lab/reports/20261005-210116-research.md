# T03 — MLB pitcher strikeout, hits-allowed and walks props

## What I did

Searched for modeling approaches, umpire/park effects, lineup-confirmation timing,
and published real-line backtests for MLB pitcher K/hits-allowed/walks props.
T02 (previous topic) had already registered the two generic "the market may miss
whiff-rate/matchup/umpire factors" (S013) and "short rest overprices overs" (S014)
claims, so this round was pointed specifically at mechanisms T02 did not cover:
catcher framing, umpire bias toward specific *batters* (not just a per-umpire
average), park/weather interactions beyond the obvious altitude story, BABIP/DIPS
theory for hits-allowed (hits-allowed itself got zero coverage in T02), the
quick-hook/batters-faced secular trend, the 2023 shift-ban rule change, and
lineup-confirmation timing. I also went looking specifically for a real, dated,
verifiable backtest against actual lines, since the standing orders ask for
evidence an edge was refuted or already priced in, not just claims it exists.
Added 8 entries (S019–S026).

## Overall picture

Hits-allowed and walks props have almost no dedicated literature at all — searches
for "hits allowed prop model" or "walks prop backtest" return the same handful of
practitioner glossary/strategy pages recommending BABIP regression and command
metrics, with zero real backtests found anywhere. The strongest material for both
markets turned out to be *generic baseball sabermetrics* (DIPS theory for hits
allowed, batters-faced-per-start decline for all three props) rather than anything
betting-specific — which is itself informative: nobody appears to have published a
hits-allowed or walks prop backtest, full stop, `claim` or better.

The best single find this round is also the most useful "other side" result:
**thecapper.io's public, dated K-prop track record (S026)** — 160-130 (55.2%) across
290 graded picks over 5.5 months, netting to **-0.1% ROI**. A win rate comfortably
above 50% still lands at roughly breakeven once real odds are applied, which is a
concrete, falsifiable data point that the strikeout-prop market is closer to
efficient against straightforward modeling than the raw win-rate headline would
suggest — exactly the kind of refutation evidence the standing orders ask for, and
rare in this space (most "track records" are marketing copy with no numbers behind
the claim at all, which is what I found when I actually opened pages for PropsBot,
PitchProphet, ModelPlay, and Rithmm — Brier-score claims but no per-market win
rate/ROI shown on the page itself).

The two peer-reviewed finds (S020 umpire star-batter bias, S024 shift-ban BABIP
effect) both come with a real caveat I want to flag clearly rather than bury: S020
is KBO data around a 2024 ABS cutover, not MLB, where ball-strike calling in 2026 is
still fully human; and S024's rule change is from 2023, three full seasons ago by
now, so any model with even a short trailing window has probably already absorbed
it. Both are genuine, well-sourced mechanisms, but neither is likely to be a *live*
2026 MLB edge as stated — I registered them because the underlying mechanism is
real and testable in principle, not because I believe either is still exploitable
today.

## Most promising testable entries

- **S023 — Trailing batters-faced projections lag the shrinking starter-workload
  trend.** The best match to data we actually have: `mlb_k_props` ships both
  `expected_bf` (the model's BF projection) and `tr_bf` (trailing/recent BF)
  alongside realized outcomes and a `month` fold column. The test is cheap and
  direct — is `expected_bf` systematically biased high relative to `tr_bf` and
  realized BF, correlated with time — and needs no new data at all.

- **S026 — the real breakeven backtest.** Also directly testable with `mlb_k_props`
  as a replication exercise: compute whether betting `p_over_model`'s edge over
  `p_over_mkt` at the posted odds nets a similarly near-zero ROI on our own data,
  which would corroborate (or contradict) thecapper.io's real-world result.

- **S021 — air density/pitch-mix interaction** is worth a partial look even though
  it is marked untestable: `mlb_k_props` already has `temp_f`, `wind_mph`, `venue`,
  and `park_k_factor`, enough to test the plain weather-vs-model-gap *main effect*
  today, even without the pitch-mix column the paper's full interaction claim needs.
  Flagged in its own skeptic_note: this paper's claimed direction (breaking-ball
  pitchers beat expectations in *low*-density air) reads as the opposite of the
  conventional Coors-Field story (thin air suppresses breaking-ball bite), so this
  is a real tension to resolve with data, not assume away.

## What would unlock the untestable entries

- **S019 (catcher framing), S020 (umpire star-batter bias):** need per-plate-appearance
  or per-start identity data (which catcher caught, batter fame/status proxy) that
  does not exist in any current dataset — `mlb_k_props` is pitcher/opponent-aggregate
  level, no catcher or individual-batter columns.
- **S022 (BABIP/DIPS), S024 (shift-ban anchoring):** both need a **hits-allowed
  prop line and odds series**, which does not exist in any dataset at all right now
  — `mlb_k_props` is strikeouts only. This is the single biggest data gap for this
  topic: there is no walks or hits-allowed market to test against, so every
  walks/hits mechanism found this round is stuck at `testable_with: []` regardless
  of how well-sourced the mechanism is.
- **S025 (lineup-confirmation timing):** needs timestamped odds history (open
  through close), not just the single closing-type snapshot `mlb_k_props` provides.

## Caveats

- The arXiv/American Statistician shift-ban figure ("~9 percentage points" BABIP/OBP
  increase for LHB) was corroborated across two independently-surfaced summaries
  before I used it, after an initial WebFetch attempt failed to parse (returned raw
  PDF bytes) — I re-fetched via the abstract page and cross-checked the number
  against a second independent source before including it, and still flagged it in
  S024's skeptic_note as not directly quoted from the primary text.
- I deliberately did not register a separate entry for the Boston University
  umpire-miscall-rate / two-strike-count-bias study, since mlb_k_props' existing
  `ump_k_factor` column already covers "umpires vary and that's priced somewhere"
  territory (S013 from T02), and a generic miscall-rate finding did not add a
  mechanism distinct enough from S020's batter-status-specific claim to be worth a
  ninth entry.
