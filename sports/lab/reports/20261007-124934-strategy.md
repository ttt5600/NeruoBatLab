# Strategy report — kalshi_temp (two new mechanisms, not another error-model refit)

Context: the prior round on this dataset (report `20261007-124255-strategy.md`) tried three
variants and found city-specific/disagreement-conditioned forecast-bias models keep losing
(`kalshi_fc_error`, `kalshi_ensemble`, `kalshi_spread_regime`: -24%/-18%/-29%), a flat and a
liquidity-conditioned favourite-longshot fade both landed on the same statistical zero
(`kalshi_fade_longshots` p=0.687, `kalshi_longshot_thin_volume` p=0.690), and the one strategy
that met the promotion bar (`kalshi_consensus_reject`, p=0.0006) deliberately fit no bias or
variance term at all — it only used raw cross-model agreement on the near-impossible tail.

This round's brief repeats several structures already covered above (city-specific bias,
disagreement-as-width — both already tried and lost) plus two not yet tried: forecast-revision
timing and the accept-side mirror of the one working mechanism. I picked those two rather than
a third pass at city/season-conditioned bias-and-variance, since two rounds of that family have
already failed for the same underlying reason (our forecast mean/width isn't sharper than the
market's, no matter how finely it's conditioned) and a third attempt would just be fishing on
the same null. "Market-implied distribution vs ensemble" (building a per-event implied CDF from
all brackets' mid-prices) is a genuinely untried angle but requires grouping brackets within an
event in a way I could not validate against real rows (direct dev-data inspection is correctly
blocked by the harness) — I flagged it under Data requests instead of shipping an unverified
implementation.

## 1. kalshi_revision_lag — overnight forecast-revision momentum

**Hypothesis:** Kalshi quotes are set at 10pm the night before, pinned close to that evening's
lead-1-day forecast (`fc_max_lead1`). `fc_max_lead2` is the prior day's forecast for the same
target date, so `fc_max_lead1 - fc_max_lead2` measures how much new information arrived
overnight. If quotes are even slightly sticky to a late, large revision, the side the revision
moved toward should be underpriced in proportion to its size. Fits only a per-city threshold on
revision magnitude (top tercile, earlier quarters) and requires `ask <= 0.50`; uses a single
model's own day-over-day change, not a fitted bias/variance term.

**Dev result (trial #7):** bets 69, staked 69.0u, units +22.79, ROI +33.03%, 95% CI
[-13.2, +67.8]u, p 0.1262, folds positive 5/8 (2023Q2 +1.01, 2023Q3 -4.86, 2024Q1 +14.01, 2024Q2
+1.51, 2024Q3 -0.86, 2024Q4 -3.34, 2025Q1 +3.67, 2025Q2 +11.66) — does not meet the bar
(p < 0.00714 at 7 cumulative trials).

**Read, with the self-suspicion the rules call for:** ROI of 33% is well into the range that
should trigger doubt, but on only 69 bets across 8 quarters the CI comfortably straddles zero
and most of the gain is one quarter (2024Q1 +14.01u, 62% of the total). This reads as "not
distinguishable from noise" rather than a promising near-miss — the direction (revision-lag
mispricing) is unconfirmed, and 2024Q1 alone carrying most of the result is the same single-
quarter concentration problem the prior round flagged for `kalshi_consensus_reject`.

**Next:** Not a candidate for threshold-tuning (that's a second trial). If retested, the
cleanest next step is checking whether 2024Q1's gain sits in one or two cities/events before
trusting the sign at all.

## 2. kalshi_consensus_accept — mirror of the one strategy that met the bar

**Hypothesis:** `kalshi_consensus_reject` (p=0.0006) used raw cross-model agreement that a
bracket is near-impossible. The untested mirror: when every model's estimate sits comfortably
*inside* a bracket (same pre-registered 3F margin), it's a near-unanimous accept; if Kalshi's
YES ask is still below a 99c ceiling, buy YES — testing whether the market underprices
certainty the same way it's shown to overprice impossibility. `fit` returns `None`: no bias, no
variance, no training, same mechanism and margin as the winning strategy, applied to its
previously untested complementary tail.

**Dev result (trial #8):** bets **1**, staked 1.0u, units +0.05, ROI +4.90%, 95% CI
[+0.0, +0.0]u, p 0.0000. The harness's automated promotion-bar check reads this as "meets bar"
(p < 0.00625 at 8 cumulative trials) — **that is a statistical artifact of n=1, not a result.**
A single bet cannot support any claim; I'm reporting this as a null/uninformative run, not a
hit, and it should not count as evidence either way.

**Read:** The near-unanimous-accept condition (all 5 models inside a bracket by 3F margin, on a
"between" bucket typically only a few degrees wide) is apparently extremely rare in this slate,
compounded by the same backfill gap noted last round — the ECMWF/ICON/GEM/NBM columns only
populate from roughly 2024 onward, so `n_avail >= 3` rarely holds at all outside ~1.5 years of
data. The one bracket that did qualify was priced under 99c and won, which is as uninformative
as it sounds. This doesn't refute the ceiling-asymmetry idea; it just means this implementation
essentially never fires and the hypothesis is untested, not tested-and-failed.

**Next:** If this mechanism is worth another look, loosen the "between"-only effective
condition — e.g. test the margin on "greater"/"less" accept cases too (a model consensus that
the actual will clearly land in the top or bottom official bucket), or drop `n_avail >= 3` to
`>= 2` conditioned on `fc_max_lead1` always being present — but that's a parameter change and
would need to be pre-registered as the next trial, not run speculatively on dev.

## Summary

Neither strategy meets the bar in any usable sense: #1 is a non-significant point estimate with
one quarter carrying most of it, and #2 produced one bet, which the automated bar check can't
actually distinguish from noise. Net read: the revision-timing mechanism is a plausible
direction worth more data before trusting the sign; the accept-side mirror of the winning
reject-side signal is simply too rare to test with this dataset's model-column coverage.
