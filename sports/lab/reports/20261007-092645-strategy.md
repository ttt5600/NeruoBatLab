# Strategy report — mlb_total, round 2 (condition / day-night / line movement)

Context: a prior round this session already tested Wrigley-specific wind, extreme
temperature tails, and umpire tendency (ledger trials #2-4, all negative, see
`20261007-092003-strategy.md`). Those three hypotheses from this iteration's focus
list are therefore not retested here. This round covers the remaining angles from the
focus list that had not yet been tried on `mlb_total`: sky condition/precipitation,
day/night x temperature, and closing-line movement. All three reuse the same NegBin
GLM scaffold as the already-tested weather model (log-link, offset = log(ou_close),
dispersion fit by NLL on training folds) so the new term sits on top of the same
controls, not a different baseline.

## 1. condition_precip_cloud — sky condition beyond temp/wind

**Hypothesis:** The weather model controls temperature and wind but not sky
condition; overcast or rainy air is denser/more humid and should suppress scoring
below what temp+wind alone predict. Added cloud and precipitation dummies (keyword
match on the `condition` string) on top of the same temp/wind controls; bet only
open-air games where one of those dummies is active and the extra term implies
EV >= 3%.

**Dev result (trial #5):** bets 208, staked 208.0u, units +6.62, ROI +3.18%,
95% CI [-21.8, +34.4]u, p 0.3186, folds positive 2/4 (2017 +13.66, 2018 -5.54,
2019 +2.15, 2020 -3.65).

**Read:** The only positive-ROI result of the seven `mlb_total` trials run this
session, but the CI straddles zero by a wide margin and p=0.32 is far from the
bar (0.01 at 5 trials). 2017 alone accounts for more than the total gain. Looks
like noise with a lucky sign, not evidence of mispricing.

**Next:** Not worth a second look at this exact specification. If retested, split
cloud and precipitation into separate bet-eligibility groups (they were pooled here
as "target") since they may have opposite-signed true effects that partially cancel
inside one EV calculation.

## 2. day_night_temp_interaction — day games compound heat beyond the main effect

**Hypothesis:** The weather model fits one shared temperature coefficient for day
and night games; day heat might compound (solar air-density/fatigue effects) beyond
what the measured temp_f and a single linear term capture. Added one `temp x is_day`
interaction on top of the same temp/wind controls; bet only day games where the
interaction term implies EV >= 3%.

**Dev result (trial #6):** bets 326, staked 326.0u, units -11.39, ROI -3.50%,
95% CI [-44.5, +22.5]u, p 0.7538, folds positive 1/4 (2017 +2.72, 2018 -3.47,
2019 -2.53, 2020 -8.12).

**Read:** Negative, one fold out of four positive, and the loss accelerates in the
most recent fold (2020) rather than reverting. No support for a day-specific heat
multiplier beyond the already-tested linear term.

**Next:** Not worth retesting day/night x temperature on raw total runs. If anything,
isolate the mechanism further upstream (e.g., does day/night predict pitcher fatigue
via pitch count, independent of temperature) rather than bundling it into one
interaction term on the total-runs outcome, which is a noisy place to look for it.

## 3. line_move_momentum — closing-line movement as a non-weather signal

**Hypothesis:** A total that moves sharply from open to close reflects
game-specific information (bullpen/lineup news) the weather channel doesn't touch;
large moves might predict the actual total continuing to drift with the move rather
than the close having already overshot it. Added one `line_move` term (coefficient
and the top-quartile |line_move| eligibility cutoff both fit on training folds only)
on top of the same temp/wind controls; bet only big-move games where the term
implies EV >= 3%.

**Dev result (trial #7):** bets 253, staked 253.0u, units -3.18, ROI -1.26%,
95% CI [-31.8, +26.7]u, p 0.5868, folds positive 1/4 (2017 -1.17, 2018 -1.43,
2019 +3.49, 2020 -4.07).

**Read:** Small loss, wide CI, no fold pattern. Closest of the three to a true null
(ROI magnitude smallest), consistent with closing-line movement already being
efficiently incorporated into `ou_close` by the time of the close, which is exactly
what "closing line" is supposed to mean.

**Next:** Not worth retesting the direct effect. A sharper version of this
hypothesis would need a feature this dataset doesn't have: *when* during the betting
window the move happened (late steam vs. a drift that settled early), since early and
late line moves carry very different information content and this test could not
distinguish them.

## Summary

None of the three meets the promotion bar (p < 0.01 at 5-7 cumulative `mlb_total`
trials, tightening with each one). Combined with the prior round, all seven
`mlb_total` trials this session are directionally consistent: closing totals look
efficiently priced against every weather-adjacent and market-structure signal tested
so far, at the dev sample sizes available (34-1867 bets depending on filter).
`condition_precip_cloud`'s positive ROI is the one result worth remembering as a
candidate for more data, not as a lead to bet on.

## Data requests

- Timestamped line history (not just open and close) would let a line_move
  hypothesis distinguish early drift from late steam, which open-vs-close alone
  cannot do.
- A finer condition taxonomy (e.g., cloud-cover percentage or a documented enum)
  instead of a free-text string would remove the keyword-matching guesswork in
  `condition_precip_cloud` and make the cloud/precipitation split less noisy.
- Pitch count or bullpen-usage-to-date would let the day/night fatigue mechanism be
  tested directly instead of inferred through a temperature interaction on total
  runs, which is several causal steps removed from the hypothesis.
