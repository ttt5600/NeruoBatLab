# T05 — NBA: rest/B2Bs, travel/altitude, injury latency, minutes, pace, referees, line timing

## What I did

Searched across rest/back-to-backs, travel and altitude, injury-news latency, minutes
projections, pace, referee assignments, and line-movement timing for the NBA. For every
claim I tried to open the primary source directly rather than trust the search-engine
summary. Added 9 registry entries (S037-S045).

**Important tooling finding, repeated from prior topics' reports but worse this round:**
the WebSearch summarizer fabricated specific details at least twice, and I caught both
only by fetching the primary page directly:
1. It invented a precise example — "Luka Doncic ruled out March 9, 2026 vs. Phoenix,
   spread moved Dallas -3.5 to Phoenix -1.5 within 14 minutes" — that does not correspond
   to any real event (Doncic was traded to the Lakers and in 2026 was ruled out of
   playoff games against the Thunder, not a Phoenix game as a Maverick). I did not use
   this anywhere.
2. It claimed a NBA Gambling Inefficiencies "Second Look" study covered 2012-13 to
   2018-19 and that NBA referee assignments post "90 minutes before tip-off." Direct
   fetches showed the actual study covers 2000-01 to 2007-08, and official.nba.com
   states assignments post at approximately 9:00am ET game day — many hours before
   tip, not 90 minutes. Both corrected numbers are what went into the registry.

Every entry below reflects the directly-fetched number, not the search summary, except
where explicitly flagged as search-snippet-only (S041's 23%/42% referee figures, where
both SSRN and SAGE full text returned 403).

## Overall picture

NBA has zero academic literature maturity compared to NFL/MLB on this project's prior
topics — most "studies" bettors cite are practitioner blogs with unsourced magnitude
claims (3-8 points for this, 2-4 points for that) and no sample size or backtest. The
real peer-reviewed literature exists but is narrower and often contested:

- **Referee bias is a live academic argument, not a settled fact.** Price, Remer and
  Stone (2012, JEMS) found home-team, in-game-trailing-team, and series-trailing-team
  referee bias, arguing all three are profit-enhancing for the league (S040). Deutscher
  (2015, Journal of Sports Analytics) used the NBA's own official assessment data and
  titled the paper "No referee bias in the NBA" — a direct rebuttal (I could only
  confirm this at the title/abstract level; both full texts were paywalled). A newer 2025
  paper (Belasen, Belasen & Olbrecht, Journal of Sports Economics / SSRN) finds a third,
  different angle — referee error rate itself correlates with how close the pregame
  betting spread is (S041) — but I could not get past SSRN's or SAGE's 403 to verify the
  specific 23%/42% figures beyond a search snippet.
- **The clearest refute-on-replication pair in this topic**: Paul, Weinbach & Wilson
  (2004, Quarterly Review of Economics and Finance) found betting the under on the
  highest NBA totals profitable (>52.4% in several bins) using 1995-96 to 2001-02 data.
  I directly fetched a follow-up study (The Sport Journal, "A Second Look") using
  2000-01 to 2007-08 — the period immediately after — and it found no significant edge
  either direction, with the raw pattern even flipping toward overs (S042). This is the
  strongest, most directly-verified decay story in the whole topic.
- **Travel/altitude effects are real but narrow and not shown against betting lines.**
  The eastward-jet-lag paper (PMC9245584, peer-reviewed, 11,481 games, 2011-12 through
  2020-21) found a specific, counter-intuitive effect — it is the HOME team's own recent
  eastward travel that hurts it, not generic visitor fatigue (S038) — with several
  p-values sitting right at the 0.05 boundary across multiple tested magnitudes/
  directions, a pattern consistent with some selective significance. The Denver/Utah
  altitude story (S039) traces to a real arXiv paper (Lopez, Matthews, Baumer) using
  betting-market data, but the specific "67% win probability" figure is press paraphrase
  and the paper itself, per Forbes's own description, does not isolate altitude from
  travel, crowd, and referee confounds — and this is such a well-known, decades-old
  effect that it is a textbook case of something probably already priced in.
- **Injury-news latency is real enough that people went to federal prison over it.**
  The 2025 Rozier/Jones/Billups indictments (S043's skeptic_note) confirm the mechanism
  — nonpublic injury/minutes information has real, illegally-exploited betting value —
  but that is evidence for the illegal, nonpublic version of the edge, not the legal
  "watch the public report and act in the book-to-book lag" version, which remains
  claim-grade with no backtest I could find anywhere.
- **Pace and referee-crew-assignment-timing entries (S044, S045) are the weakest in the
  topic** — real, well-documented public facts (pace is rising; crews post ~9am ET) but
  every magnitude number attached to them in practitioner content is unsourced.

## Testability

Every single entry in this topic is `testable_with: []`. This project has no NBA dataset
— `nfl_spread`, `nfl_total`, `nfl_moneyline`, and `mlb_k_props` are the only schemas that
exist. Nothing here can be tested now regardless of how strong the underlying claim is.

## What would unlock these entries

- **All nine entries** need, at minimum, an NBA game-level dataset with closing
  spread/total lines and odds, comparable in structure to `nfl_spread`/`nfl_total`.
- **S037 (rest)**: additionally needs days-of-rest for both teams per game.
- **S038 (jet lag), S039 (altitude)**: additionally needs a team travel log (origin city,
  time zones crossed, days since last game at a given elevation) — not derivable from
  team identity alone the way NFL travel distance can be looked up from a static city
  table, since it depends on the specific road-trip sequence.
- **S040, S041 (referee bias)**: need play-by-play foul calls with game-clock/score
  context and officiating crew identity — far beyond any closing-line schema; S041
  specifically needs Last Two Minute report error counts by team.
- **S043 (injury latency)**: needs a timestamped, multi-sportsbook odds feed plus
  timestamped official injury-report status changes, not a single closing snapshot.
- **S044 (pace), S045 (referee crew)**: need team/crew-level pace and foul-rate stats
  joined per game, plus (for S045) both a morning-of-assignment line and the closing
  line to see if any shift happens after the 9am posting.

## Caveats

Two WebFetch attempts on PDFs (the arXiv jet-lag replication-style Lust thesis, and the
Subperfect Game ResearchGate PDF) returned binary/garbled content the fetch tool could
not parse; I relied on search-result titles and abstracts for those, graded accordingly,
and did not invent numbers to fill the gap. Several SAGE/ScienceDirect/SSRN pages
returned 403 and are flagged in their respective `skeptic_note` fields as unverified
beyond title/abstract or search-snippet level.
