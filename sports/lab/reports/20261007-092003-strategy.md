# Strategy report — mlb_total park-specific weather/umpire tests

Focus: the league-wide weather effect (temp, wind) is already ~95% priced into the
close (see `mlb_weather_glm`, ledger trial #1, ROI -5.43%, p 0.85). This round tests
three narrower, park- or crew-specific hypotheses the market may underweight even
though the average weather adjustment is efficient. No registry entry is tagged
`mlb_total` (the 13 testable entries are all NFL or `mlb_k_props`), so all three are
agent-originated, each stated and run once with no threshold sweeps.

All three reuse the same NegBin GLM scaffold as the already-tested weather model
(log-link, offset = log(ou_close), dispersion fit by NLL on training folds) so that
the new term being tested sits on top of the same controls, not a different baseline.

## 1. park_wind_wrigley — Wrigley-specific wind interaction

**Hypothesis:** Wrigley's small, exposed outfield has a wind sensitivity the
league-wide model (one shared wind coefficient across all parks) averages away. Added
one `wind_signed x is_Wrigley` term on top of the global temp/wind controls; bet only
Wrigley games where that extra term implies EV >= 3%.

**Dev result (trial #2):** bets 63, staked 63.0u, units -3.32, ROI -5.27%,
95% CI [-18.3, +12.1]u, p 0.6672, folds positive 1/4 (2017 -3.42, 2018 -2.96,
2019 -2.04, 2020 +5.10).

**Read:** No signal. 63 bets over 4 seasons is already a small sample for one
ballpark, and 3 of 4 folds lost. The famous "Wrigley wind" is also one of the most
talked-about park effects in baseball media — if anything is already priced by
books, this is a good candidate for it.

**Next:** Not worth a second look at Wrigley alone. If park x wind is retested, it
should pool several small/open parks with a shared shrinkage prior rather than
hand-picking the single most famous one, which likely has the least residual
mispricing precisely because it's the most talked-about.

## 2. temp_tail_nonlinear — extreme heat/cold hinge terms

**Hypothesis:** The weather model fits temperature linearly; scoring may bend at the
tails (>=95F loosens grip and carries fly balls further, <=40F the reverse) beyond
what a straight line captures. Added two hinge terms (knots at 95F and 40F) on open-air
games only, on top of the same linear temp/wind controls; bet only tail games where the
hinge implies EV >= 3%.

**Dev result (trial #3):** bets 34, staked 34.0u, units -4.08, ROI -12.01%,
95% CI [-16.3, +7.8]u, p 0.7474, folds positive 2/4 (2017 +1.00, 2018 -7.00,
2019 +2.91, 2020 -1.00).

**Read:** Negative and the smallest sample of the three (34 bets across 4 seasons of
every MLB game — true tail games, ex-dome, are rare). Not enough bets to say anything
about whether the nonlinearity is real; this result is closer to noise than evidence
either way.

**Next:** This hypothesis needs more seasons before the tail bins have enough games to
separate signal from noise — not a good candidate to retest on the same dev window.

## 3. umpire_total_tendency — empirical-Bayes umpire shift

**Hypothesis:** Home-plate umpires differ in strike-zone size, which moves walk rate
and pitch counts and therefore scoring. Crews are known well before the close but the
posted total doesn't key off the umpire. Shrunk each umpire's training-fold
mean(actual - close) toward the league mean (prior strength k=40 games, picked in
advance as roughly a season of starts) and used it as a mean shift in the NegBin model;
bet only umpires with >=30 training starts where the shrunk effect implies EV >= 3%.

**Dev result (trial #4):** bets 1867, staked 1867.0u, units -22.44, ROI -1.20%,
95% CI [-107.6, +61.1]u, p 0.6984, folds positive 1/4 (2017 +22.91, 2018 -12.44,
2019 -14.17, 2020 -18.75).

**Read:** Largest sample of the three by far (1867 bets) and the smallest per-bet ROI
loss (-1.2%), consistent with a real but fully-priced (or nonexistent) umpire effect
rather than a noisy small-sample result. One fold (2017) was strongly positive but the
other three were not, and the sign flips fold to fold — looks like noise around zero,
not a trend reversing.

**Next:** Of the three, this is the one with enough bets to trust the null result.
Not worth retesting the same specification; a different cut (e.g. umpire effect
restricted to the K-rate-driving low/high strikeout zone, which is a sharper mechanism
than raw total runs) would be a new hypothesis, not a variant of this one, and should
go through `mlb_k_props` instead where umpire K-rate factors (`ump_k_factor`) already
exist.

## Summary

None of the three meets the promotion bar (p < 0.0125 at 4 cumulative `mlb_total`
trials). All three point the same direction as the original weather GLM: closing MLB
totals look efficiently priced against the weather- and crew-level signals tested so
far on this dataset, at the dev sample sizes available (34-1867 bets depending on how
narrow the filter).

## Data requests

- A second book's total line per game (SBR currently gives one closing line) would let
  a line-shopping or disagreement-based strategy be tested, which none of the four
  hypotheses here could touch.
- `mlb_total` carries `officials` as a single home-plate umpire name but not the rest of
  the crew; it also has no umpire K/BB factor analogous to `mlb_k_props`'s
  `ump_k_factor` — porting that feature over would let an umpire hypothesis use a sharper,
  pre-validated mechanism (zone size -> K/BB rate) instead of a direct fit on noisy total
  runs.
- No ballpark wind-rose / orientation data (true compass heading of each park's outfield
  axis) is available; `wind_dir` is already game-relative ("Out To CF" etc.) which helped
  here, but there is no feature distinguishing "small, exposed" parks from "large,
  sheltered" ones other than hand-picking by name, which concentrates risk on a few
  famous and likely well-priced cases (as it did with Wrigley above).
