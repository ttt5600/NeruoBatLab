# Strategy report — kalshi_temp (structure beyond forecast recalibration)

Context: three prior trials on this dataset (`kalshi_fc_error`, `kalshi_ensemble`,
`kalshi_fade_longshots`) all tried to price brackets from a per-city forecast-error
model and lost -24%/-18%, or broke roughly even on a flat favourite-longshot rule.
The instruction for this round was explicit: the market's forecast is sharper than
both a GFS-only model and a 4-model ensemble, so don't just re-fit another global
error model. All three strategies below were designed so that none of them re-fits
an absolute bias/level forecast — they either hold the mean fixed and only let width
vary with a new conditioning variable, condition an existing bias rule on liquidity,
or skip modeling entirely and act on raw cross-model consensus.

## 1. kalshi_spread_regime — disagreement-conditioned width, no bias term

**Hypothesis:** Cross-model forecast disagreement (ensemble spread across GFS,
ECMWF, ICON, GEM, NBM) is a same-day uncertainty signal a flat per-city width
ignores. Keeps mu = ensemble mean (no fitted bias — that's what already lost twice)
and only lets the pricing sd depend on which pooled disagreement tercile today's
spread falls in, fit on earlier quarters. Bets EV>=5% sides only in the top/bottom
disagreement tercile, skipping the middle tercile where a flat model should be fine.

**Dev result (trial #4):** bets 4690, staked 4690.0u, units -1348.33, ROI -28.75%,
95% CI [-1569.2, -1115.7]u, p 1.0000, folds positive 0/4 (2024Q3 -200.87, 2024Q4
-252.34, 2025Q1 -438.32, 2025Q2 -456.80).

**Read:** Worse than either prior error-model attempt, and monotonically worse each
fold. Letting sd float with same-day ensemble spread did not recover the edge —
if anything it made the EV screen fire more confidently (and more often: 4690 bets,
far above the other two error-model trials) on exactly the days it should have been
most cautious. This reads as further, stronger evidence for the standing finding:
our forecast inputs (even disagreement-conditioned) are not sharper than the
market's price at any horizon tried so far, not a new mechanism.

**Next:** Not worth another variant of "recondition the error model on X." Any
future forecast-based attempt on this dataset needs a signal that is not already a
transform of the same handful of GFS/ECMWF/ICON/GEM/NBM columns, or should be
abandoned in favor of pure market-structure signals like strategies 2-3 below.

## 2. kalshi_longshot_thin_volume — favourite-longshot bias x liquidity

**Hypothesis:** Favourite-longshot bias (ledger: `kalshi_fade_longshots`, p=0.687,
closest to breakeven of anything tried) should be strongest where it doesn't pay
anyone to arbitrage it away: thin-volume brackets. Restricts the existing
YES-ask<=5c fade-NO rule to the bottom tercile of same-city `volume_to_decision`
(tercile fit on earlier quarters) instead of firing on every cheap YES regardless
of liquidity — a liquidity-friction mechanism, not a re-run of the same rule.

**Dev result (trial #5):** bets 893, staked 893.0u, units -1.90, ROI -0.21%,
95% CI [-9.7, +5.0]u, p 0.6904, folds positive 5/12 (2022Q3 +0.29, 2022Q4 -0.74,
2023Q1 -1.51, 2023Q2 +0.30, 2023Q3 -0.54, 2023Q4 +0.08, 2024Q1 +0.09, 2024Q2 -0.88,
2024Q3 -0.26, 2024Q4 -0.57, 2025Q1 -1.14, 2025Q2 +2.97).

**Read:** Statistically indistinguishable from zero — essentially the same
breakeven read as the unconditional fade rule, just on a third of the bet count.
The liquidity filter didn't concentrate the bias one way or the other; it mostly
just subsampled a flat result. No evidence arb capacity is the missing ingredient
in the favourite-longshot story here.

**Next:** Not worth a volume-threshold sweep (that would be tuning on dev). If this
mechanism is retested, a cleaner version would use bid-ask width on the bracket
itself (yes_ask - yes_bid) as the staleness proxy instead of volume, since volume
and liquidity-provider attention aren't necessarily the same thing on Kalshi.

## 3. kalshi_consensus_reject — raw cross-model agreement, no training at all

**Hypothesis:** When every available forecast model's point estimate sits outside a
bracket's range by more than a 3F safety margin, the bracket is a near-unanimous
reject regardless of any individual model's bias or the ensemble's average error.
If Kalshi's YES ask is still priced above a 1c floor on such brackets, buy NO. `fit`
returns `None` — no bias, no variance, no training step, so this cannot be a
disguised re-fit of the error model that already lost twice.

**Dev result (trial #6):** bets 1259, staked 1259.0u, units +27.96, ROI +2.22%,
95% CI [+11.3, +44.9]u, p 0.0006, folds positive 4/6 (2024Q1 -0.04, 2024Q2 -1.02,
2024Q3 +3.80, 2024Q4 +0.10, 2025Q1 +16.93, 2025Q2 +8.18) — **meets the promotion
bar** (p < 0.00833 at 6 cumulative trials on this dataset). This is a dev
hypothesis, not a finding; only the lockbox, scored by a human, can call it a
result.

**Read, with the caveats that matter before anyone gets excited:**
- Only 6 of the 16 dev quarters produced any bets at all, and they're the 6 most
  recent (2024Q1-2025Q2). The ECMWF/ICON/GEM/NBM member columns are presumably not
  archived before then (same backfill gap `kalshi_ensemble`'s docstring already
  flagged), so this result has no evidence from 2021-2023 — it is really a 1.5-year
  test wearing a 4-year dataset's p-value.
- Of the +27.96u total, +16.93u (61%) comes from 2025Q1 alone and +8.18u (29%) from
  2025Q2; 2024Q1-Q2 are flat-to-negative. Two quarters are carrying the result.
- ROI is modest (2.22%, not the >15%-on-hundreds-of-bets range that should trigger
  self-suspicion), and the mechanism doesn't require trusting our forecast accuracy
  at all — only that 5 independent model providers agreeing a bracket is far
  outside their collective range is informative regardless of their individual
  skill. That's a more defensible prior than anything in strategies 1 or 2.

**Next:** Before trusting this past the lockbox: (a) get the full 2021Q3-2023Q4
history for the ensemble member columns if it exists anywhere, to see whether the
signal held up before 2024 or whether this is two good quarters; (b) check whether
2025Q1/Q2's gain concentrates in one or two cities or one or two anomalous weather
events, which would be a different, weaker claim than "consensus-reject is
generally mispriced." Do not re-tune the 3F margin or 1c floor on dev — that's a
second trial on the same idea and would need to clear the next, tighter bar.

## Summary

1/3 meets the promotion bar, and it's the one strategy that didn't try to improve
on the forecast at all — it only used the fact that 5 independent models agreed a
bracket was far outside their collective range. The disagreement-conditioned error
model (#1) was the worst result yet on this dataset, reinforcing rather than
challenging the standing finding that our forecast inputs can't out-price the
market. The liquidity-conditioned longshot fade (#2) landed on the same breakeven
null as the unconditional version. Read #3's p=0.0006 as "worth a human look before
the lockbox," not as settled — it's backed by 1.5 years of data and two
quarters carry most of the profit.
