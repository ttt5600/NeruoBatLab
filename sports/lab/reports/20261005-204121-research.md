# T01 — Market efficiency and documented biases (NFL/NBA/MLB/NHL)

## What I did

Searched peer-reviewed and working-paper literature on the classic sports-betting
anomalies — favourite-longshot bias, home/away bias, overreaction, recency, anchoring —
across the four major US leagues, specifically asking what survives at **closing**
lines in **recent** data. For every claimed edge I searched for a refutation, a decay
result, or a methodological critique. Added 9 registry entries (S001–S009). Most paper
PDFs (ScienceDirect, SAGE, Springer) returned 403/paywalled; where I could not open the
full text I relied on search-result snippets and said so explicitly in the
`skeptic_note`, rather than inventing numbers.

## Overall picture

The single most important finding is methodological, not a specific bias: Vandenbruaene,
De Ceuster & Annaert's 2022 *Journal of Sports Economics* review of over 600 tested
spread-betting strategies found only ~3% remain "profitable" once a strict
multiple-testing correction (|z| > 3) is applied — most of the literature's claimed
inefficiencies are consistent with efficient markets plus data mining. I didn't log this
as its own registry entry (it's not a tradeable strategy), but it should discount every
other entry's headline number, and I folded it into several `skeptic_note` fields
directly.

Within that frame, a clear pattern recurs across sports: biases that are real tend to
be present in **opening** lines and gone by the **close** (MLB favourite-longshot bias,
S002; NFL letdown/clinched-team bias shrinks substantially open→close, S004), or they
were real in an earlier era and have since decayed (NFL home-underdog bias has fallen
from ~58% in 1973-79 toward ~52-54% by the 2000s-2010s, S003; NHL totals inefficiency
found in an earlier small-sample study essentially disappeared in a later, larger-sample
study, S008). The most direct 2026-dated evidence (S009) is a new preprint that tested
the Levitt "sportsbooks price to exploit known bettor bias" hypothesis on real MLB
run-line closing data from this year and found the public-bias pattern present but *no*
detectable mispricing beyond the standard hold — a clean, recent refutation of treating
that mechanism as a live edge today.

## Most promising testable entries

- **S003 — NFL late-season home-underdog bias.** Borghesi (2007) found the NFL's
  aggregate home-underdog overperformance is concentrated almost entirely in week 15
  onward. Directly testable with `nfl_spread`/`nfl_moneyline`: `week`, `spread_line`,
  `home_ml`/`away_ml` identify underdog status, and the hidden outcome columns give
  ATS/ML results. Worth testing because the skeptic evidence (secular decline in the
  raw home-underdog win rate, and the independent, betting-unrelated decline in NFL
  home-field advantage itself since ~2019) gives a sharp, falsifiable prediction: if the
  effect survives at all in 2006-2019 data, it should show up concentrated in weeks
  15+ and should look weaker in the later seasons of the sample.

- **S005 — NFL point-spread overreaction/recency bias.** Classic finding (1981-1995
  data) that lines overweight a team's last 1-5 games relative to longer-run ability.
  Directly testable with `nfl_spread`/`nfl_moneyline`: `home_form_margin`/`away_form_margin`
  (short-window form) vs. `home_elo_pre`/`away_elo_pre` (longer-run rating) vs.
  `spread_line` lets you check whether the line moves further toward recent form than
  the rating gap would justify. I could not confirm the 2021 follow-up paper's effect
  size (403 on fetch), so this is really "test the 1981-1995-era hypothesis on 2006-2019
  data" rather than reproducing a specific modern number.

- **S009 — Fade-the-public-favourite (not currently testable, but worth flagging).**
  Not testable with our datasets (needs ticket/handle-% by side, which none of our
  schemas carry), but the 2026 MLB run-line refutation is the most current, most
  directly relevant data point in this whole topic to whether *any* of these classic
  "bettors are biased, so fade them" stories still cash today. If handle/ticket-percentage
  data is ever added to a dataset, this becomes the highest-value re-test.

## What would unlock the untestable entries

- **S002, S009 (MLB moneyline/run-line open-vs-close, public bet-% by side):** need
  historical MLB moneyline/run-line odds with timestamps (open through close), plus
  ticket-count or handle-percentage by side — the latter is the hard part, it's not
  typically public and is the actual mechanism Levitt's hypothesis depends on.
- **S004 (NFL letdown/clinched-playoff bias):** needs a weekly standings/clinching
  feed (clinched any berth, clinched a top seed, eliminated) joined to game-level
  spreads — a derived feature, not currently in `nfl_spread`.
- **S006 (NFL anchoring to preseason odds):** needs preseason Super Bowl futures odds
  by team/season — not in any current schema.
- **S007 (NBA momentum/streaks), S008 (NHL totals):** need an NBA and an NHL
  game-level dataset respectively (closing lines + odds + results); neither sport has
  a dataset in this project yet.

## Caveats

Several sources (ScienceDirect, SAGE, Springer-gated pages) returned 403 and could not
be opened directly; those claims are marked as snippet-sourced and unverified in the
relevant `skeptic_note` rather than presented as confirmed. The NBA S007 entry in
particular (56.5% ATS win rate, 13 straight winning seasons) is flagged as suspiciously
clean relative to Camerer's (1989, AER) much more cautious finding that the NBA
momentum effect is real but too small to exploit, and relative to the Vandenbruaene
multiple-testing critique — it should not be taken at face value without reproduction.
