Can it find the call types on its own?

The clustering number in the record was AMI 0.5475 at k=16 — but k was chosen by maximizing agreement with the human labels. That answers a different question than the one people ask. Choosing k blind, and dropping k-means, gives a better number honestly.

Adjusted mutual information against the eight human call-type labels. Layer-3 mean-pooled 768-d embeddings, L2-normalized. Shuffled-label null: 95th percentile 0.0017 over 500 permutations.

The problem

A number that answered the wrong question

Sweeping k and reporting where agreement peaks is a fair measurement of how much call-type structure exists. It is not an unsupervised result. Someone clustering an unlabeled corpus has no way to know to ask for sixteen clusters — the labels supplied that.

Splitting the two questions apart changes both answers. Choosing k from the geometry alone turns out to cost almost nothing; the clusterer, which nobody had questioned, turns out to cost a great deal.

Going fully unsupervised costs 0.018 AMI. Switching off k-means gains 0.133.

Finding 01

k-means was the wrong clusterer

Ward agglomerative clustering beats k-means at every k tested, and the gap is largest exactly where Ward is strongest — a 0.133 margin at k=9.

Ruling out optimization luck

Ward is deterministic and k-means is not, so a single k-means seed against Ward is not a fair fight. Twenty seeds, plus one 200-restart run:

Ward beats the best of twenty seeds and the 200-restart run. It is also not that k-means is optimizing the wrong thing: the correlation between inertia and AMI is negative at every k (−0.36, −0.47, −0.66), meaning a better k-means fit genuinely does recover call types better. The optimizer is working. Even its global optimum is worse.

What fails is the model. k-means assumes isotropic, comparably-sized blobs; the call types differ 3× in frequency and each is a curved manifold of renditions rather than a ball. Ward only assumes that greedily merging the pair that least increases within-cluster variance is a reasonable move — a much weaker commitment.

Ward is also less contaminated by bird identity at the same k — 0.167 against k-means' 0.190 — so it wins on both axes at once.

Correction to the record. The published 0.5475 is the maximum over 20 k-means seeds. The mean is 0.5326 (sd 0.0092) and a 200-restart run gives 0.5354. Seed 0 with n_init=10 drew the top of the distribution — about 0.015 optimistic. If k-means is quoted at all it should be 0.535 ± 0.009.

Finding 02

Choosing k without looking at the labels

Six criteria, none of which ever sees the labels. Only two of them behave.

Calinski–Harabasz falls monotonically from k=2; Davies–Bouldin and BIC are still improving at k=40, the edge of the sweep. All three are uninformative on this corpus and must not be quoted as recommendations — "BIC says 39 call types" would be an artifact of where the sweep stopped, not a finding.

Stability is the one that works. It asks a harder question than the compactness measures: cluster two random 80% subsamples independently, then compare their labels on the clips both saw. Structure that is real reproduces; a k that is slicing through a continuum does not. Its top values for k≥5 are k=13 (0.816), k=7 (0.813), k=6 (0.812) — an interior optimum, landing inside Ward's broad plateau.

Stability scores k=3 at 0.976, far above everything else. A three-way split of almost any dataset is trivially reproducible, which is why the k≥5 floor is stated out loud rather than quietly applied.

Finding 03

Is HuBERT earning its keep?

An AMI of 0.6 means nothing until a dumb method has been tried on the same clips.

HuBERT's partition is not merely duration in disguise: the AMI between the HuBERT clustering and the duration clustering is only 0.199 at k=8, against 0.395 between HuBERT and the spectrogram.

Still missing: a random-init HuBERT control — the sharpest test of whether pretraining, rather than the convolutional stack's built-in spectral bias, did the work. It needs torch, which the local analysis environment lacks. This is the top open item and belongs on Savio.

Finding 04

Four types come out clean. Four collapse.

The aggregate number hides the shape of the result. Reading the Ward partition against the taxonomy shows the model is not uniformly mediocre across eight categories — it is excellent on half of them and blind on the other half.

Four of the eight types are recovered with no labels at all. Distance splits into two clean clusters, which is a candidate sub-type worth listening to. The other four — Nest, Thuk, Tuck, Whine — form one confusable block; all three of Thuk, Tuck and Whine have their largest cluster dominated by Nest.

These are the same four types the supervised probe fails on. So it is a property of the representation, not of the clustering algorithm.

The probe's per-class recall runs Song 0.990, Distance 0.964, Aggressive 0.949, Tet 0.832 against Thuk 0.573 and Tuck 0.379. Unsupervised and supervised methods fail on the same boundary, which leaves two readings that this experiment cannot separate: either the model genuinely cannot hear the distinction, or the distinction is partly annotator convention.

Finding 05

A clean core, and a caveat that matters

HDBSCAN picks its own cluster count and is allowed to refuse a clip. At min_cluster_size=20 it independently lands on nine clusters — the same count as Ward's peak, from an entirely different density-based model — and scores 0.774 on the 40% it does assign.

That 0.774 is not comparable to the full-set numbers — it is measured on whatever the method found easy. Two checks decide whether it means anything.

It is not just Song: excluding Song entirely, core AMI is still 0.763 across 1007 clips. But splitting the core by cluster size is damning:

The pockets are one bird's Songs, one bird's Tets, and so on. They score well on call type only because a single bird's pocket happens to contain a single call type. Ward on the same 1116 clips at k=9 gets 0.739 against HDBSCAN's 0.774, so most of the apparent advantage is the subset, not the density model.

Practically: an auto-labeling tool built on this would need to merge the voice pockets first. And the core is badly unbalanced — 72.7% of Distance calls are assigned but only 12.1% of Tucks — so it would not give even coverage of the taxonomy.

Finding 06

Layer choice is not something you can get right blind

Layer 3 is the published default, chosen by a supervised probe. For clustering it is best at k=9 (0.609) and second at k=13 (0.591); layers 0–3 are the good region and the deep layers are clearly worse, with layer 9 bottoming out at 0.486.

But the ranking within the shallow group flips depending on k, so the honest phrasing is "an early layer, 0–3" rather than "layer 3".

The limitation worth stating. No label-free criterion identifies layer 3. Silhouette decreases monotonically with depth (0.167 at layer 0 → 0.115 at layer 10), so it always picks layer 0 regardless of merit; stability picks layer 10 at k=9 and layer 0 at k=13. Layer choice needs a small labeled validation set. That belongs in the README rather than being papered over.

Caveats

What would change these numbers

  • Mean-pooling across a 365× duration range. Clips run 0.032 s to 11.705 s. A Song is averaged over roughly 585 frames, a Tuck over about 3. Those are not comparable summaries, and it likely explains both duration's strength as a baseline and Song's trivial separability. A duration-matched or segment-level analysis would test it.
  • Pretraining overlap. These clips were inside the pretraining corpus. Pretraining was label-free, so no call-type mapping could have been memorized, but absolute numbers may be optimistic. The duration and spectrogram baselines share the same clips, so the comparisons hold regardless.
  • No random-init control yet. Without it we cannot separate what pretraining bought from what the convolutional stack's spectral bias gives for free.
  • AMI on a self-selected subset — the HDBSCAN core — is not comparable to full-set AMI, and is reported separately above for that reason.

Reproduce

Everything here runs on a laptop

No GPU and no torch. The scripts read the cached run11_layersweep.npz; only the baselines script also needs the clip wavs.

cd release/zf_hubert_run11/experiments

python unsup_calltype.py      --kmin 2 --kmax 40 --n-boot 12  # main sweep, ~25 min
python unsup_kmeans_vs_ward.py                                # fairness check
python unsup_baselines.py     --ks 8 9 16                     # duration + spectrogram
python unsup_ward_anatomy.py                                  # cluster composition
python unsup_hdbscan_core.py  --min-cluster-size 20           # core vs voice pockets
python unsup_layer_sweep.py   --ks 9 13 --n-boot 6            # layer choice

Requires numpy, scipy and scikit-learn ≥ 1.3 for HDBSCAN. Raw output for every number on this page is in experiments/results/.
