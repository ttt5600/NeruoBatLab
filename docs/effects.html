What our preprocessing and corpus choices actually bought

Eleven manipulations, ranked by measured effect on the same corpus. The largest is not the corpus, the target, or the architecture — it is how audio is handed to the encoder. Two of the biggest levers are preprocessing decisions that were never the subject of an experiment.

The one-line summary

Preprocessing dominates. Simply encoding clips at their own length instead of padding them to a common one is worth +0.478 accuracy — more than pretraining on 116 hours of zebra finch buys over a random encoder (+0.194). Waveform normalisation moves the holdout more than any corpus change we have run. Meanwhile the label-target choices we spent the most compute on move things by about ±0.02.

1 · Normalisation: the lever nobody chose

matched = run11-style per-chunk normalisation. native = raw waveform, the condition AVES trained in.

23 of 23

Matched normalisation beats native on the BirdPark holdout for every model measured, without exception — median +0.0405 AP, from +0.0013 to +0.1916. In distribution the same switch is worth +0.0000 AUC across the pretrained baselines. It is nearly free to get wrong in-sample and expensive to get wrong out-of-sample.

That asymmetry has a consequence, because the pre-committed arm is chosen on in-distribution ZF AUC. For the DAPT checkpoints — which inherit AVES's raw-waveform training condition — native genuinely fits better in distribution, so the rule picks it: buying 0.0067 AUC and paying 0.0861 AP. A 13× bad trade, made automatically, every time.

The sharpest case

dapt1e4_step15000 scores BirdPark AP 0.8479 under matched normalisation — above run11's 0.8121 — and 0.7052 under native, below the 0.7472 log-energy floor. The same weights are either the best model here or a failed one, depending only on this switch. It is recorded as a failure.

2 · Padding: the largest effect in the project

What happens when variable-length clips are batched to a common length. Accuracy RISES as each defect is removed, so the ledger entry is the gain from dropping the padding, not a benefit of it.

Armaccuracy
notebook full0.3478
minus rectify0.2484
minus padding0.8261
minus weak head0.8696
leave birds out0.7928

The median clip is 121 ms, padded to 15.7 s: 99.2% of what the encoder sees is zeros. Mean-pooling over all ~782 frames then averages mostly padding, and the embeddings collapse together — mean pairwise cosine 0.964 with padding against 0.735 without. Nearly identical vectors cannot be told apart by any classifier.

This is the same mechanism behind the standing rule never to batch variable-length audio through these encoders: group normalisation runs over time, so padding corrupts short clips rather than merely diluting them.

Three things worth stating plainly. MFCC really is worse (+0.0226 against run11's spectrogram, same sign at both k) — that decision was right. But run11's 4000-d soundsig spectrogram is not the best target even within its own family: a 384-d log-mel scores +0.0141 higher at one tenth the dimensionality. And AVES's iteration-2 recipe scores worse here, not better (-0.0224) — so AVES's advantage does not come from its labels being better aligned with calls on this corpus.

A number to stop quoting

target_quality.json reports the k=100→200 effect as −0.0208, but it measured that on a log-mel carrier it mislabels as "run11's actual recipe". On run11's real feature the k penalty is -0.0070 — three times smaller. Finding 040 has the feature identified correctly; the decomposition in target_quality does not.

The practical consequence is not small. Storage for the combined corpus was budgeted at ~604 GB because the target is 4000-d. A 384-d log-mel target is ~10× smaller and scores higher on call identity — which would have put the whole corpus inside ~60 GB.

Two monotone trends run through the stack. The representation gets steadily higher-dimensional — 45 components carry 90% of variance at the CNN, 95 by layer 11 — and the first principal component gets steadily more correlated with loudness, from 0.540 to 0.667. Linear separability barely moves across the same range (0.946 → 0.957), so the extra dimensions are not buying detection accuracy; the dominant axis is increasingly just volume.

That is the mechanistic version of the loudness result below, and it is a reason to prefer shallow layers that has nothing to do with accuracy: the decoder-free analysis picked layers 0–2 for both encoders, and this is what layers 0–2 are avoiding.

What this ledger is for

Ranked together, the pattern is hard to miss: how audio is prepared and handed to the encoder moves results more than which corpus it was pretrained on. Padding, normalisation and input representation occupy the top three places. The corpus and target-recipe questions that consumed most of the compute budget sit near the bottom at ±0.02 — real, reproducible, and an order of magnitude smaller than a batching decision made in passing.

Every figure read programmatically from ~/zf_labelset/zf_detection_dataset_v1/analysis/*.json; none typed by hand. Harvest at paper/effects/effects_harvest.json. Effects are not on a common scale — call-type accuracy, detection AUC, BirdPark AP and label AMI answer different questions — so the ranking compares magnitudes within each metric's own context, not across them. The BirdPark holdout resolves about ±0.06 AP, so individual deltas below that are not meaningful on their own; the normalisation claim rests on the consistency of its sign across 23 paired models, not on any single delta.
