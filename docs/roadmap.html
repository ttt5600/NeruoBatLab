Encoder Roadmap

Every pretraining variant we have run, are running, or intend to run — laid out as one design space so no cell gets tested twice and none gets missed. Current state (Oct 8): our best models and every AVES checkpoint sit within one noise band, and one extra seed moves a model by half of it, so the next phase measures before it builds.

00 Where we are

Six controlled runs since Oct 4 settled the label and corpus questions, and showed that our measuring stick, not the model, is now the limit. Every number below is on the model board.

Seed luck is about 0.015

The same recipe, trained twice with different random seeds, landed 0.0155 apart on call type. That is half the width of the band our best models and every AVES checkpoint now share.

0.8379 run16 · seed 1337 → 0.8224 run16 · seed 2

11-class call type, held-out birds. Difference −0.0155, 95% [−0.0289, −0.0017], lower at all 12 layers (finding 057).

What held up. General audio alone (run20, 0.8370) matched the mixed corpus and beat zebra finch audio alone (run21, 0.8151, which is resolved below run16 by −0.0229 [−0.0383, −0.0079]). For learning zebra finch call types, varied audio mattered more than colony audio (finding 061). Labels made by AVES gave our best model so far (run19, 0.8464), level with the six AVES checkpoints (0.845–0.855). It is still not distinguishable from run16 (+0.0085 [−0.0127, +0.0293], finding 059).

What did not. Labels made by our own run16 (run18, finding 056) and tripling the general-audio replay in continued AVES training (finding 060) changed nothing we can measure. The earlier "4× compute closed the gap" (finding 052) does not reproduce at seed 2: run16 seed 2 is not distinguishable from run15 (+0.0088 [−0.0099, +0.0282]).

So the next phase measures first. More seeds, a standard benchmark with many independent tasks (BEANS, which includes Egyptian fruit bats), and a label-free score. Only then do we combine what worked or decide to build on BirdAVES and move to bats.

Selection amplifies noise 50–70×

Across a whole training trajectory, in-distribution AUC moves 0.002 while holdout AP moves 0.14. The criterion used to pick an arm carries almost no information about the axis we care about — so arm spread gets reported alongside every holdout number, and post-hoc maxima never get quoted as results.

05 Hard constraints

Things no amount of compute will fix. Worth stating so they stop being re-proposed.

AVES's corpus is ~70 % unobtainable

Of its 360 h, only FSD50K (~108 h) is distributed as audio. AudioSet and VGGSound ship YouTube IDs, and the AVES paper states outright that the corpus is not exactly reproducible. Links have rotted since 2022. An exact replication is impossible, and any scraped substitute would differ in unknowable ways.

One cross-lab holdout, four blocks wide

BirdPark is the only encoder-level holdout that is both clean and cross-lab. It can detect a failure the size of round-1 DAPT; it cannot rank checkpoints within a trajectory. The chick recordings were built out as a second axis and returned not distinguishable on every comparison — they are same-corpus, so they do not test domain shift. Real resolution needs a larger cross-lab recording set.

Nothing above 8 kHz exists for these models

Every model here — ours and AVES — takes 16 kHz audio, so the top of the spectrum is discarded before the network sees it. Three FSD50K clips were rejected as silent purely because all their energy sat above 8 kHz. For bats, whose echolocation lives far above that, this is the first design decision, not a detail.

No bird is held out of pretraining

99.5 % of curated clips come from the 120 pretrained recordings, so leave-birds-out constrains the probe, not the encoder. Every in-distribution number carries this caveat.
