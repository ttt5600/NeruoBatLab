Encoder Roadmap

Every pretraining variant we have run, are running, or intend to run — laid out as one design space so no cell gets tested twice and none gets missed. Current state (Oct 4): more compute closed the call-type gap to AVES once, then stopped helping — so the next lever is the training labels, and run18 tests exactly that.

00 Where we are

The project's long-running result was that every zebra-finch model we trained lost to AVES — the same architecture, pretrained on generic animal audio. Since Sep 22 four controlled runs narrowed that to one lever that worked and one that stopped working.

Compute helped once, then stopped

Same corpus (ZF + FSD50K, 224 h), same labels, same 93,750 steps. The only change between these three runs is how much audio each training step sees.

0.8136 run15 · 80 s/step → 0.8379 run16 · 322 s/step → 0.8321 run17 · 644 s/step

11-class call type, held-out birds; majority class 0.1797. run16 − run15 = +0.0243, 95% [+0.0099, +0.0419].

The honest reading. run16 is the first model of ours that no AVES checkpoint beats by more than noise on call type — but every AVES point estimate is still ahead, by 0.007 to 0.017, so this is not a tie. run17 then fit its training labels better (lower loss at every stage) and scored call types worse at all 12 layers. Getting better at predicting spectrogram-cluster labels stopped translating into understanding calls, which points at the labels. AVES paired its compute with iteration-2 labels (re-clustering a trained model's layer 6); ours were only ever tried at low compute. run18 is that test, and its label step currently stops at a safety gate: the new labels cover 32 fewer frames than the old ones (out of 40.4 M), so they are not handed to training until the cause is found.

On the cross-lab BirdPark holdout, dapt-replay (AVES weights + 19 % general audio during continued training) remains the best arm, and it is still the only one better somewhere and worse nowhere.

03 How every model gets judged

One protocol, fixed in advance. The layer and normalisation are chosen by out-of-fold in-distribution AUC and frozen before any holdout is touched.

Selection amplifies noise 50–70×

Across a whole training trajectory, in-distribution AUC moves 0.002 while holdout AP moves 0.14. The criterion used to pick an arm carries almost no information about the axis we care about — so arm spread gets reported alongside every holdout number, and post-hoc maxima never get quoted as results.

05 Hard constraints

Things no amount of compute will fix. Worth stating so they stop being re-proposed.

AVES's corpus is ~70 % unobtainable

Of its 360 h, only FSD50K (~108 h) is distributed as audio. AudioSet and VGGSound ship YouTube IDs, and the AVES paper states outright that the corpus is not exactly reproducible. Links have rotted since 2022. An exact replication is impossible, and any scraped substitute would differ in unknowable ways.

One cross-lab holdout, four blocks wide

BirdPark is the only encoder-level holdout that is both clean and cross-lab. It can detect a failure the size of round-1 DAPT; it cannot rank checkpoints within a trajectory. The chick recordings were built out as a second axis and returned not distinguishable on every comparison — they are same-corpus, so they do not test domain shift. Real resolution needs a larger cross-lab recording set.

Nothing above 8 kHz exists for these models

Every model here — ours and AVES — takes 16 kHz audio, so the top of the spectrum is discarded before the network sees it. Three FSD50K clips were rejected as silent purely because all their energy sat above 8 kHz. For bats, whose echolocation lives far above that, this is the first design decision, not a detail.

No bird is held out of pretraining

99.5 % of curated clips come from the 120 pretrained recordings, so leave-birds-out constrains the probe, not the encoder. Every in-distribution number carries this caveat.
